Info

Every explanation on the site, in one place — what the tracker watches, how the rating moves, and what each page is for.

The tracker

Where the results come from

A bot watches live team-mode lobbies around the clock. It waits for the match to finish, works out which team won, and updates everyone who played. It never joins as a playing ship. It only watches.

Player names come straight from the game itself, exactly as they are spelled — nothing is read off the screen any more, so unusual names are no longer misread.

Which matches get watched

Only {{ capacity }} matches are watched at a time, across every region. A match is picked up once it is {{ min_age_mins }} minutes old — games rarely end before then, so watching earlier would waste a slot — and a lobby needs a few players and to be under {{ max_age_mins }} minutes old to be worth one.

A game that never gets tracked counts for nobody.

The rating

Your skill rating

Everyone starts at {{ starting_elo }}. A match moves you by at most {{ elo_k }} points either way.

How much you move depends on who you beat. Winning against a stronger side gains close to the full amount; beating a much weaker side gains almost nothing. Losing works the same in reverse — losing as the favourite costs the most, losing to a much stronger side costs almost nothing. Two evenly matched teams move by exactly one point.

A team's strength is the average of its two highest rated players, not everyone on it. The practical effect: farming weaker teams will not inflate your rating — climbing means beating people who are themselves rated highly.

Full elo and half elo

Be in a match before the tracker starts watching it and it counts in full. Join one already being watched and it still counts, at half — on either side, winners and losers alike. What matters is whether you were in the match when the watch began; there is no deadline beyond that. The Play list marks which is which before you join.

Quitting does not dodge a loss

A losing team is charged as its fullest roster, not as whoever was still there at the end — leaving early when a match turns bad does not avoid the loss.

And a player who finishes on exactly 0 score is not rated at all, win or lose. Being in the lobby is not the same as playing.

How a winner is decided

A match is only scored once its lobby actually closes. A team is out when its station is destroyed and it has no players left — a damaged station alone means nothing; teams play on with damaged stations. If one team remains, it wins. If station damage cannot separate the survivors, the higher team score decides.

When the result genuinely cannot be determined, nothing is recorded. A missing match is better than a wrong one.

The leaderboard

One combined board, and one per region

The combined view shows one rating per player — the number every region has been feeding. Each region is also its own leaderboard, because a rating earned against one continent is not the same achievement as one earned against another; matches played in a region count towards that region's board.

On the combined board the Region column shows where each player turns up most — click it to open that region's leaderboard at that player's own row. Their profile has the full split if they play in more than one.

Why a profile's regional splits may not add up

The per-region breakdown on a player's profile is counted from stored match history, which began on 7 August. For anyone already on the board before then, those rows will not add up to the totals at the top of their profile.

Time windows

All time shows the rating itself. The 30-day, 7-day and 24-hour views rank by rating gained in that window, because a rating is one running number and cannot be cut into days or weeks — so somebody who arrived yesterday and won can outrank a long-standing name who sat out.

The green tick

A ✓ beside a name marks a confirmed rating: that player has Protection on, so only matches they checked into count.

Play

Checking in

Press Play on the match you are about to join and it counts for your account — whatever name you end up playing under. Pressing Play notes the moment you clicked; the next ship to appear in that lobby under the name you play as is taken to be you, and from then on you are matched to that ship rather than to the spelling — so two players sharing a name no longer interfere.

Keep the in-game name on the Play page the same as the one in your ship, or there is nothing to match you to.

Your name rides the clipboard

Pressing Play also copies your in-game name, so you can paste it straight into the name box when Starblast opens. The name cannot travel in the link itself: Starblast fills its box from its own browser storage and ignores the address, and no site is allowed to write another site's storage — so pasting is as close as this can get.

Names, accounts and claims

Two names, two jobs

Your account name is how you appear on the leaderboard and how other players recognise you — so after it is first set it can only be changed once, in Settings. Changing it keeps your whole record; it cannot be used to shed a bad rating.

Your in-game name — the one in your ship — is set on Play and you can change it as much as you like. Results always go to your account, whichever name you played under.

Your rating belongs to your signed-in account (Google or Discord). Being on the same network as someone else counts for nothing.

Claiming a name that is already on the board

If a name on the leaderboard is really yours, claim it rather than picking a new one — claiming keeps everything that name has already won. It becomes yours once it wins {{ wins_required }} tracked match{{ '' if wins_required == 1 else 'es' }} after you file the claim. The claim is shown publicly on that player's page while it is open, so the real owner can see it and report it.

Protection

Only count the matches I check into

If someone plays under your name, turn on Protection in Settings. Your rating then rests only on games you confirmed by pressing Play first, and nobody wearing your name can move it. The leaderboard marks it with the green tick.

It can only be flipped once every 24 hours. Without the cooldown it would be a switch to throw the moment a match looked like going badly — off while winning, on to void the losses. The rating only means something if the choice is made in advance and lived with.

When results go missing

Why a win might not show up

The match was not watched. Only {{ capacity }} lobbies are watched at once, and a match nobody was watching cannot be scored afterwards.

The result was unclear. If the tracker cannot tell who won, it records nothing rather than guessing.

You finished on 0 score. Those are never rated, win or lose.

You are using a default name. Names the game hands out are shared by many players, so they are never rated.

Two people had your name and neither checked in. Pressing Play matches you to your ship, which is exactly the fix.

Clans

Switched off for now

A clan tag used to be guessed from the front of a player's name, and those guesses were frequently wrong. Clans come back once there is enough real match history under the new system — membership will then be something a clan admin confirms, so a player joins because their clan added them, not because of how their name is spelled.

If you run a clan and want it set up when this reopens, say so to {{ contact }} on Discord.

Privacy

What the site knows about you

If you sign in: an opaque account number from Google, or your Discord id and username (Discord sign-in asks for the narrowest scope Discord offers, and the bot needs the handle to recognise you). The names you register or play under, your match history and scores. That is the complete list.

What it does not keep

No IP addresses. Not when you press Play, not when you sign in, not on reports or claims. Everything stored before this policy has been wiped. Rate limiting — the only thing addresses were ever used for — now works on an irreversible coded tag whose key is kept outside the database, and the tags expire within two days. There is no query anyone can run against this database that turns a player into an address.

No email addresses, no passwords, ever. Sign-in happens on Google's or Discord's own pages; this site never sees a password, and the email inside Google's sign-in token is discarded, not stored.

The honest caveat

The web server this site runs on keeps standard access logs — visitor address next to URL, with no player name attached — for about a week before they delete themselves. Every website on the internet has these, Starblast included, and they cannot be switched off on this hosting plan. What matters is that nothing ties an address to a name, here or anywhere.

Reporting a problem

It is read by a person

Something broken, a result that looks wrong, or an idea — send it from the Report page. What helps most: the name you were playing under and roughly when the match was; whether you pressed Play on that match first; and for anything on the site, what you clicked and what happened instead.

Sign in before sending if you can — a report with an account attached can be answered, and checked against your matches.

Questions? Ask {{ contact }} on Discord.
